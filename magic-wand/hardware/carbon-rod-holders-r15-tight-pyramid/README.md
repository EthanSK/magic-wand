# R15: tighter pyramid key holder

R means revision. R15 follows R14, retaining two centred upper keys above three lower keys in the same plane. It closes the row gap, corrects the reference cap orientation and recesses the lower edge. R14 remains preserved.

## Layout and interfaces

This revision retains the existing Choc V1 switch/socket interfaces and inferred 17.5 x 16.6 mm caps. Saker Mini switches or 18 x 18 mm caps need a separate fit and spacing revision; these projects do not qualify them.

- Lower centres: X -18.7, 0, +18.7 mm, Y 0. Upper centres: X -9.35, +9.35 mm, Y 17.8 mm. Two keys face toward the tip by default.
- The reference cap orientation is corrected. In the sourced Choc V1 switch mesh the two 1.2 x 3.0 mm posts are 5.7 mm apart along X, and [Adafruit's 5737 underside photo](https://www.adafruit.com/product/5737) shows both stem sockets along the cap's wider side. The reference is therefore 17.5 mm along X and 16.6 mm along Y. Earlier references put the longer side on Y. Under the new inferred orientation, R14's nominal X gap would be about 0.9 mm before sideways play, rather than 1.8 mm; this is not a measurement of the bought caps.
- Compared with R14, lower pitch widens 0.3 mm (18.4 to 18.7), row spacing closes 1.5 mm (19.3 to 17.8) and each upper key moves 0.15 mm outward. The nominal reference-cap gap is 1.2 mm on both axes.
- Cap size is inferred, not measured. The 17.5 x 16.6 mm comes from [Adafruit's same-family 5111 listing](https://www.adafruit.com/product/5111); the 5737 page gives no dimensions, and a scaled photo estimate (about 18.2 x 17.3 mm) is unreliable. Independent 3 mm strokes and the same provisional 0.4 mm allowance on every cap side leave a 0.4 mm residual gap. Measure a real cap mounted on a switch before printing: if either side exceeds 17.5 (X) / 16.6 (Y) mm, or sideways play exceeds 0.4 mm per side, widen the pitch first. These numbers are screening assumptions, not manufacturing tolerances or physical noncontact evidence.
- Lower front edge (Ethan: "Frame edge below the bottom row"): every part of the carrier stops at Y -7.9, 0.4 mm behind the inferred fronts of the three lower caps; R14 projected to Y -13.8. The bumper face cannot move further back because it carries the switch flange land to Y -7.5. One continuous bumper replaces the round front rail, deep front webs and leg ends. Its front face runs from Z 8.7 to 10.8, with a softened top edge and outer corners. Below that, a 45-degree chin recedes 1.7 mm to Z 7.0, ending 2.1 mm behind the cap fronts. That chin is the finger relief this front-down print can make without supports; a concave groove would need supports on the bed face. A shelf below the switch housing, Z 7.0-8.7 back to Y -4.5, restores depth behind the chin and rejoins the front clip ties. No rail head, leg or tie end pokes below the chin.
- The front clip moves from Y -12 to -6.1, flush with the front face, so the ring no longer projects where the index finger wraps the rod. Its fixed crown, between the two ties, has a 0.5 mm 45-degree front-edge chamfer. The free spring arms, bore, gap and friction profile are unchanged. Above the centre clip, between the crown and the chin, a V-shaped notch opens 3.15 mm at the front face. Two flat-fronted diagonals tie the clip to the fixed rails, and a tapered spine joins its crown to the centre socket floor. Comfort is unmeasured.
- A tapered fixed spine grows from inside the rear clip crown into both upper socket floor fronts, above the rod and below every upper switch/socket route. The upper-left floor no longer starts as a free tongue, and the right floor no longer starts as an island on its aid.
- Keep five bought switch/socket interfaces, the separate sliding battery above toward the tip, external wires, upward USB and almost-closed slide-on clips. Adjacent fixed frames can merge; complete hardware and insertion spaces are cut from the final joined solid so a neighbour cannot fill an opening.
- The battery holder, gate and sensor retain R14 geometry. Exact final mesh comparison and whole-project checks are recorded in AUDIT.md.

## Reproduce

Use the pinned requirements environment and Bambu Studio CLI. Run each finite CAD/export/check unit serially through the repository-root launcher: `python3 .agents/skills/printability-audit/scripts/geometry_budget.py --timeout-seconds 900 -- /path/to/cad-python <script-and-arguments>`. This owns timeout/cancellation cleanup and complete query batches, without a hard RAM limit or host-pressure gate. Run `cad.py`, `verify_geometry.py`, `compare_revision.py`, `layout_views.py`, `print_project.py --slice --scratch <fresh-external-scratch>`, then `verify_slice.py` and `strength_audit.py`. Build the first trial with `print_project.py --slice --only Key_Carrier --scratch <another-fresh-external-scratch>` and run the slice/strength checks with `--exports exports/trial-Key_Carrier` plus its exact `--project` for the slice checker.

Run `test_start_audit.py`, each project's `review_views.py` and `reopen_project.py --exports <directory> --scratch <fresh-external-scratch>`, then `audit_views.py`. Review every new overhang/warning before updating snapshot-specific decisions; never reuse an old decision against a new row. Run `qualify.py` for each project, `build_preview.py`, `check_delivery.py`, and root `npm run check`. Keep large temporary slicer data on verified external scratch.

## Physical trial

Before printing, mount a real cap on a switch and measure it. Confirm its longer side runs along the stem posts (X); measure both sides and the sideways play. Widen spacing if they exceed 17.5 x 16.6 mm or 0.4 mm play per side. Helper-contact faces sit 0.07-0.1 mm off Bambu's layer-middle planes; still check the preview for the empty layer under the rear clip and each socket floor. After cooling, hold the fixed frame and cut/peel helper walls outward in short sections. The key carrier no longer has bonded ties; cut the sensor holder's labelled fixed-rail ties. Never lever a spring, socket floor or bearing face. Stop on fused helpers, damage, whitening, cracks or excessive force. Helper release remains unverified.

Inspect every functional opening, floor and bridge. Slide clips from a bare rod end. Fit all five real switches, sockets and caps without forcing. Press every key and neighbouring combinations through full travel with ordinary sideways movement; no cap contact is acceptable. Inspect all rails, joints, floors and posts after repeated presses and judge upper-key reach, grip and twisting. Hold it normally with the index finger at the lower edge: check for poking edges or front-clip contact, and inspect the bumper, chin, front ties and spine after pressing. Firm upper-key presses must not lift or open the front clip; moving it toward the keys raises its lever share.

Only after the carrier passes, proceed to the cage, gate and sensor trials: inspect bridges, pouch-safe cell loading and retention, empty gate cycling by its centre bar, populated-board underside and upward USB/external lead clearance. Adhesion, sag, cleanup, fit, breaking force, fatigue, battery retention and comfort remain physically unverified. A digital audit cannot guarantee that no printed part will snap.

Narrow nonfunctional slits between already-joined fixed rails can fuse; do not pry them open. Functional bores, clip gaps and hardware routes still require clearance. Inspect the entire upper floor starts for sag; actual partial backing is quantified in AUDIT.md.
