## User design rules

- Preserve the original counter-wound helix / criss-cross pattern and recognisable wand silhouette. Reducing mass means thinner walls and hollow interiors; it does not authorize removing decoration or replacing the shape.
- The shaft body must taper continuously towards the head. No pinched waist that widens again, and no external bulb/collar added solely for a joint. Reinforce joints internally. The diamond naturally widens above its matching narrow neck.
- A complete Bambu project always contains **both left- and right-hand handles**. One shared set of shaft/head parts is acceptable, as in Print V2; state the contents clearly. A left-only file may be an explicitly labelled optional subset, never the complete delivery.
- Preserve purchased switch/socket holes and their alignment when reducing the surrounding insert. Recess compact mounts into the grip; use the purchased caps rather than adding visible printed squares or cap covers.
- Preserve board retention, upward USB access, battery solder/wire clearance, cable routes and handed button positions unless the user changes them.
- The current Shard R2 loop candidate uses radial hook entry, a 4 mm upward slide into solid load stops, and a separate indexed quarter-turn keeper. Insert the keeper fully, turn it 90°, then pull outward 0.65 mm to seat it; press in before turning back to release. Remove the battery cap during handle entry/removal. This replaces the failed whole-loop cam and flexible catch. Preserve its final orientation, butt anchors, band and both hands; verify the whole route and whole locked assembly. See the latest repair section below. Physical fit and strength remain unverified. User-corrected — 2026-10-28.
- When the loop is removed, the stem must remain completely usable as a normal wand, with no projecting brackets, receivers, rails or release tabs. Put the receivers inside the existing surface and keep projecting pegs and release hardware on the removable loop. Inspect and verify the bare-wand state alongside the attached state in both hands. V14’s external cam housings were rejected despite its working geometric lock; retain that model only for history. User-corrected — 2026-10-27.
- Keep prior models and delivered files unchanged. New revisions and supporting files go to Downloads; editable sources and regenerated exports are committed together under the repository rules.

## Fixed ergonomic priorities

Read the frozen [V20 design principles](../../../magic-wand/hardware/printed-loop-wand/v20/design-principles.json) before the next revision. They define the butt reference, lower mount, mount spacing and button offsets. Preserve these relationships until the user explicitly changes them; do not derive a new hand position from a convenient connector location.

- Keep the removable finger loop at the butt, with only the clearance needed for the cap and release motion. Removing an unwanted tail must not lift the whole handle higher up the wand. V18 failed this: preserving its high mount positions was the wrong priority.
- Keep all three buttons positioned relative to the loop as one ergonomic group, mirrored for both hands. Moving the loop requires moving the group by the same amount. Preserve button angles and their relative height spacing.
- The loop is a slim solid band, not a thick hollow tube. Keep reinforcement local to the locking ends. Do not preserve obsolete tubular cavities or perforations after this explicit change. The current V20 band is 9 × 4 mm with 1.4 mm rounded edges and gradual 23 mm flares into the wider fitted pads. A request to widen/smooth the loop does not authorize changing its butt anchors, button layout or compatible locking geometry.
- A requested 5–10 mm grip reduction is a substantial size change, not another 1 mm adjustment. Report measured axes honestly. Keep battery space and the wire route, and disclose a wider battery section rather than claiming a circular diameter reduction where none exists.
- Locking interfaces serve the approved grip position and silhouette. Do not silently sacrifice those priorities to reuse old pegs, cap dimensions or joint heights. Verify the cap, buttons, cell, wires and complete attachment path after resolving the packaging.

## Before calling it final

User-requested — 2026-11-10: “slicing is super quick, so we should do it regularly at intervals to make sure that the 3D model is going into plan.” Slice at useful checkpoints during design, especially after changing thin members, openings, print orientation or adhesion helpers, so actual layer problems are caught before the final pass. Reuse unchanged checked results; do not wait until a long design pass ends to inspect the first real toolpaths.

Before finishing every model or print project, use the repository's [printability-audit](../printability-audit/SKILL.md). It owns the recurring independent Opus review of actual holes, support removal and likely snap points, with minimal design changes. Keep implementation and independent review distinct.

Map each requested change to a source parameter or feature and a check. Compare old/new assembled views, not just the print plate. Check the core-radius profile for monotonic taper; explicitly check the helix is generated. Inspect both handed handles and count both in the actual 3MF build. Check new joints inside the chosen silhouette instead of making the silhouette fit arbitrary collars.

Run manifold, wire/board/USB, joint motion and lid capture checks as applicable. Slice in the actual PETG setup and compare like-for-like material estimates, including supports. Support contact blockers do not alone stop tree branches or descending normal-support columns entering hollow passages; block their landing-source regions where needed and inspect sliced paths. Separate CAD clearance, slicer evidence and physical print acceptance. If the battery product is still unknown, retain its provisional status rather than inventing a fit.
